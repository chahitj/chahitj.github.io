Under-canopy monitoring

A crop-monitoring rover has to pass between rows, carry sensors, communicate under canopy, and remain inexpensive enough to replicate. I worked on the physical platform and the autonomy, sensing, and communication needed to test that idea.

The first mechanical constraint was practical: a reference rocker-bogie chassis was too wide for the intended crop rows. I reworked its geometry in Fusion 360 rather than treating the reference as a finished rover, then carried that narrower layout through custom motor mounts, sheet-metal fabrication, and packaging for compute, batteries, wiring, and sensors.

Rover design and fabrication

Mechanical design and fabrication

I narrowed a rocker-bogie reference architecture for crop-row clearance and designed the chassis and motor mounts around the parts we needed to package. I then carried the Fusion 360 assembly into bent sheet-metal hardware, adjusting battery space, wiring access, sensor placement, and serviceability during assembly.

Compute and sensing architecture

I integrated motor controllers, power, Raspberry Pi 5 compute, ultrasonic sensors, IMU, and radio around a serviceable physical layout. Cost and field repairability shaped the component choices and wiring access.

LoRa communication and localization

I modeled LoRa coverage under assumed canopy attenuation and prototyped anchor-based TDoA localization for GPS-degraded operation. In field tests, we placed radio nodes and sensors on poles and one node near a warehouse, then sent bidirectional request–response messages to observe reception, loss, signal strength, and link range. Localization remained at the prototype stage.

Swarm autonomy architecture

I developed ROS 2 obstacle-avoidance, row-tracking, coverage, and formation behaviors and worked through the package, model, environment, and dependency bring-up needed to run them in Gazebo. Obstacle avoidance and row tracking were tested on the physical prototype; coverage and formation remained primarily simulation work.

Sensing, communication, and autonomy

The physical platform combines motors and microcontrollers with ultrasonic and IMU sensing, Raspberry Pi 5 compute, and LoRa communication. Obstacle avoidance and row tracking ran on the prototype; coverage and formation behaviors were developed mainly in Gazebo.

System integration

Sensing, state estimation, behavior, and communication were integrated around the fabricated rover.

  1. 01Measure the environment

    Ultrasonic sensing and IMU measurements feed the state-estimation and obstacle-avoidance work.

  2. 02Turn state into behavior

    Kalman-filter sensor fusion supports ROS 2 behaviors for avoidance, coverage, and formation keeping.

  3. 03Close the loop on hardware

    Motors and microcontrollers move the fabricated chassis; LoRa supports communication investigated in separate field tests.

Field communication tests

The first field measurements happened early in the growing season, when canopy was sparse; we later tested under denser canopy. The request–response tests recorded message reception, loss, signal strength, and link range as placement and canopy changed.

The Monte Carlo coverage model used assumed dense-canopy attenuation to explore anchor geometry before field measurements. The heatmap below shows that simulated estimate. I also prototyped anchor-based LoRa TDoA localization for GPS-degraded operation, where arrival-time differences between fixed anchors constrain possible rover positions.

Physical tests covered prototype obstacle avoidance and row tracking. Gazebo supported the broader coverage and formation work; the full multi-rover autonomy stack remained a simulation and development effort.

LoRa coverage feasibility

The Monte Carlo model estimates how many anchors could hear a rover across a 300 × 180 m field under assumed canopy losses, providing an anchor-placement study before broader field measurements.

Anchor-based TDoA localization

Fixed anchors receive one rover transmission at different times. Those differences give range-difference constraints for a localization prototype in GPS-degraded conditions.

Failure analysis

From prototype to field tests

The width constraint forced a mechanical redesign before autonomy could matter. Early radio tests established a sparse-canopy baseline; later tests moved into denser foliage. GPS degradation motivated the TDoA prototype, while fleet-scale coordination and coverage remained simulation and development work.
