Autonomous racing

An autonomous racecar has to choose a driveable path from limited, noisy sensing while leaving enough time to steer and brake. I implemented several planning and control methods in C++ and ran them on the physical F1TENTH car.

One repeated crash was especially instructive: the planner steered toward an opening that existed in its range scan but not in the physical track.

Reactive gap following

On hardware, Follow-the-Gap processed the LiDAR scan into contiguous free-space regions and selected a steering target. I blended the deepest point with the gap center and used steering-dependent speed tiers. An instantaneous time-to-collision braking layer provided a separate safety response.

Autonomy methods

Selected simulation clips show reactive planning, sampling-based search, and trajectory optimization at different levels of the stack.

Planning and control on the car

Algorithm progression

PID wall following and Pure Pursuit established feedback and path tracking. RRT/RRT* explored sampling-based routes; SLAM-based planning and trajectory optimization handled map-aware motion. I used simulation to inspect each method and deployed selected controllers on the physical car.

Follow-the-Gap with steering blend

The controller clips invalid LiDAR returns, smooths the scan, places a safety bubble around the nearest obstacle, and finds a contiguous gap. A blend of gap center and deepest point sets the steering target; tighter turns use lower speed tiers. Physical tuning added a disparity extender and additional speed limits.

Physical-car integration

I integrated LiDAR-based planning on the physical car and tested it on the barrier-lined track. The iTTC layer applied emergency braking when a forward obstacle's estimated collision time fell below 0.8 s.

Sensor-aligned debugging

The car repeatedly turned toward the same apparent opening even though the track was closed there. I compared the steering behavior with the LiDAR view, covered the reflective tube or wall region with a matte black jacket, and repeated the run under darker room lighting. Both interventions changed the behavior and pointed to missing or unstable returns at that surface.

Physical-car testing

Physical-car clips document Follow-the-Gap and PID on the lab track. For the final race, our two-person team used my faster reactive approach and placed second among ten teams.

Physical-car runs

Two controllers were tested on the physical F1TENTH racecar: Follow-the-Gap (reactive) and PID (baseline). Both ran on the lab track with inflatable barriers.

Failure analysis

The phantom LiDAR gap

Unreliable returns at the reflective region looked like free space to the gap selector, so the controller steered toward a wall. Covering the surface with matte material and changing the room lighting changed the behavior, which localized the failure to a sensing-and-surface interaction.

Reflectance debugging and phantom-gap mechanism

The physical test isolated a sensing hypothesis. The schematics trace how unreliable LiDAR returns could present an apparent opening that Follow-the-Gap selected.
