Throughput under congestion

In lifelong MAPF, agents receive new goals as they finish old ones. At high density, PIBT can lose throughput when traffic concentrates in narrow warehouse corridors.

I tested whether a corridor-preserving region graph could guide PIBT without sacrificing its local collision handling. I compared matched maps, densities, and seeds.

Capacity-preserving decomposition

The corridor-split decomposition kept 84 physical channels separate. A max-flow check confirmed 21 node-disjoint paths at both grid and region-graph levels.

Same map, different guidance

Two approaches on the Paris map at 3,000 agents produce visibly different traffic patterns. The videos compare behavior; the controlled throughput results below use matched maps, densities, seeds, and task streams.

Preserve the corridors, not just connectivity

The region abstraction preserves distinct corridors so guidance can represent their capacity.

Region-guided planning and reproducibility

Region-guided PIBT

The region layer estimates occupancy and biases PIBT's next-step candidate ranking toward a route interface. PIBT still handles local collision resolution.

Integration and experiment checks

I integrated the external Guided-PIBT C++ baseline and built a Python harness that recorded maps, agent counts, seeds, and results. State checks halted experiments when a reproduced run diverged.

Region-guided PIBT method pipeline

  1. 01Corridor-split region graph

    Preserve obstacle-separated channels in the region graph.

  2. 02Live region occupancy

    Track agents per region at each timestep.

  3. 03EWMA congestion estimate

    Smooth short occupancy spikes.

  4. 04Congestion-weighted routing

    Bias routes toward lower estimated congestion.

  5. 05PIBT candidate ranking

    Favor the next region interface in PIBT's candidate order.

Experiment and reproduction checks

  1. 01Record the inputs

    Tie maps, densities, seeds, and tasks to a manifest.

  2. 02Check reproduction

    Halt when manifests or state checksums diverge.

  3. 03Analyze surviving runs

    Keep the 203-region study separate from later experiments.

Warehouse experiments

On the primary warehouse at 2,000 agents, vanilla PIBT, region guidance, and external Guided-PIBT completed 3.44, 5.74, and 9.38 tasks per step.

The C++ region-guided planner ran at 10.7–19.6 ms per step versus 28.9–32.0 ms for external Guided-PIBT, but remained behind it in throughput.

Failure analysis

Where the hypothesis broke

The gain did not generalize reliably. The best ratio to vanilla PIBT was about 0.91× on a larger warehouse and 0.43× on a random map. Corridor-preserving boundaries avoided the clustering caused by uniform and spectral decompositions, but did not solve generalization.

A later pilot failed the stored-run reproduction check and was stopped before interpretation. In the retained 203-region study, the proposed critical-density signal did not predict collapse, so I rejected the hypothesis. Region-PP also remained harmful after its implementation bug was fixed.

Where the improvement does not generalize

The method helped on warehouse-10, but its best ratios to vanilla PIBT fell to approximately 0.91x on warehouse-20 and 0.43x on random-32.
